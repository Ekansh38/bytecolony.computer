This is a technical writeup on how the CPU functions, starting with basic circuits and finishing with a simple CPU.

The article was written for readers who have heard the phrase "computers think in ones and zeros" many times but never seen the actual chain of reasoning that turns a wire into arithmetic, a relay into memory, or a table of instructions into a running program. It builds up piece by piece — a switch, a light bulb, a relay, a logic gate, an adder, a register, a fetch–decode–execute loop — with a custom-drawn diagram for nearly every step.

The goal was to sit between the two extremes of existing material on the subject: the high-level explanations that skip the mechanism, and the textbook treatments that are dense enough to scare most people off. The writeup is roughly a 20-minute read and assumes no prior background in electronics or computer architecture.
